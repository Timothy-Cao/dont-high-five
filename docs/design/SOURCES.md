# Research ledger

Reviewed **2026-09-21**. Store pages establish advertised mechanics, not measured fun. Developer text describes intent. Player discussions are dated anecdotes, not representative surveys. No comparable game was played during this pass. Original proposals elsewhere in this notebook are design inferences, not claims that sources prove they work.

## S01

[LOCKDOWN Protocol — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2780980/LOCKDOWN_Protocol/) — **full page read, primary marketing description**. Continuous first-person action, shared timed objectives and a hidden minority opposing them. Transfer: keep action and conversation simultaneous. Do not transfer exact tasks, weapon balance or current player count as our own requirements. Our test: three physical delivery circuits with one saboteur.

## S02

[Among Us — Innersloth, how to play](https://www.innersloth.com/games/among-us/) — **full page read, developer rules summary**. Cooperative task victory and accusation coexist with sabotage and hidden killing. Transfer: a clear public team objective and understandable antagonist interference. Omit constant meeting interruptions in the first movement test. Official gameplay image link was opened but the image could not be retrieved reliably; no HUD measurements are claimed.

## S03

[First Class Trouble — developer concept](https://www.firstclasstrouble.com/about) — **full page read, developer vision**. Progress requires cooperation despite uncertainty about companions. Transfer: helpful contact creates the opportunity for deception. Do not adopt every elimination ritual or identity system. Our test: players should voluntarily choose a partner after experiencing one useful exchange.

## S04

[Project Winter — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/774861/Project_Winter/) — **full page read, current description**. Team survival and hidden opposition place trust in an environment with shared needs. Transfer: travel and rescue can produce alibis and vulnerability. Avoid importing a survival/crafting economy that distracts from the hands. Older community complaints are not assumed to describe the current 2.0 build.

## S05

[Unfortunate Spacemen — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/408900/Unfortunate_Spacemen/) — **full mechanics section read**. Advertises randomized rescue objectives, pack hunting, a disguised monster, and proximity voice. Transfer: good players can proactively defend and investigate. Omit full shape-shifting in our first test because silhouettes, rapid movement and glove interactions already demand attention.

## S06

[Dale & Dawson Stationery Supplies — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2920570/Dale___Dawson_Stationery_Supplies/) — **full mechanics section read**. Work, apparent work and managerial suspicion form a non-monster social loop. Transfer: a suspicious action should have an innocent purpose. Example adaptation: a genuine charge inspection and a delaying tactic use the same wash station. Do not add a permanent manager who decides everyone’s play.

## S07

[Grapple Tournament — developer FAQ](https://www.grappletournament.com/faq) and [official overview](https://www.grappletournament.com/) — **full pages read, developer movement commentary**. Air steering, ledge handling and vertical combat are explicit. The FAQ cautions that huge spaces and long grapples can produce searching, hiding and difficult pursuit; some reasoning is VR-specific. Transfer: measure encounter frequency and escape chains. Do not copy VR speeds, controller aiming or comfort settings into keyboard/mouse design. Strong counterexample to “bigger and faster is always better.”

## S08

[PEAK — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3527290/PEAK/) — **cooperation/mechanics section read**. Helping partners over ledges and placing support equipment ties friendship to traversal. Transfer: a helper changes the route or outcome. Do not require complex ragdoll climbing in this controller. Proposed adaptation: recipient-controlled stored spring after a high-five.

## S09

[Webbed — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1390350/Webbed/) — **page read**. Web connection and swinging sit at the center of the premise. Transfer: one connective verb can serve movement and object manipulation. Avoid persistent player-built web structures until multiplayer authority and cleanup have been tested. The game’s 2D presentation does not establish first-person input feel.

## S10

[SpiderHeck — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1329500/SpiderHeck/) — **mechanics section read**. Web movement, ceiling travel, dangerous weapons and short physics encounters reinforce one another. Transfer: let a movement decision alter an encounter. Do not transfer one-hit elimination into a long hidden-role round; its social downtime cost would be different.

## S11

[Deceive Inc. — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/820520/Deceive_Inc/) — **mechanics section read**. Disguises, access, gadgets and extraction create multiple approaches. Transfer: equipment changes which route is possible and what an observer can infer. Omit a large character roster and elaborate NPC mimicry. Our proposed visible access cartridge should imply capability, not prove allegiance.

## S12

[Blood on the Clocktower — official rules explanation](https://wiki.bloodontheclocktower.com/Rules_Explanation) — **full rules page read**. Dead participants retain conversation and a limited vote; information can be wrong under defined rules. Transfer: protect social participation after failure and bound uncertainty. Do not copy unlimited dead-player knowledge into a physical stealth game. Our first experiment uses temporary downing rather than unrestricted ghost scouting.

## S13

[Bopl Battle — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1686940/Bopl_Battle/) — **page read**. Combining a small set of powerful physical abilities creates unexpected interactions. Transfer: test combinations of a few verbs before accumulating abilities. Do not transfer unrestricted time/scale manipulation into a first online prototype. The matching low-cost experiment is spring assist + brake + carried cargo.

## S14 — player friction, not a balance specification

[Lockdown weapon-luck discussion, December 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2780980/discussions/0/4635988976587566983/) — **thread read**. The opening player reports weapon access deciding encounters, unprovoked teammate killing, and long spectating after an early death. Replies also describe learned routes, disarming and grouping as counterplay. This is a useful disagreement: “pure luck” is not established, but the perceived agency problem is real for that participant. Local test: give every role a defensive action; measure whether new players understand why they lost. Do not describe the thread as current consensus or current patch behavior.

## S15 — useful lead, limited retrieval

[SpyParty — developer tutorial article](https://www.spyparty.com/2018/02/28/the-new-spyparty-tutorial/) and [developer FAQ](https://www.spyparty.com/faq/) — **search excerpts only; full pages returned redirect loops**. Lead: teach the same suspicious action from both sides. Proposed adaptation: let a player perform, witness and inspect one corrupted transfer in a controlled tutorial. Do not claim the complete tutorial was inspected or borrow exact timing.

## S16

[Lethal Company — official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1966720/Lethal_Company/) — **page read**. A shared scavenging objective creates reasons to enter dangerous spaces. Transfer: carry visible useful things through the environment. Omit a separate monster ecosystem and expanding economy in the first deception test. This is an adjacent cooperative reference, not proof that hidden roles improve its loop.

## S17 — secondary lead, not corroborated

[Deceive Inc. player discussion about objective incentives](https://www.reddit.com/r/DeceiveInc/comments/12gdm0k/) — **search excerpt; full page unavailable**. Raises the possibility that avoiding objectives is optimal. Treat as a question, not a conclusion about that game. Our independent failure test: can camping the exit outperform interacting with any circuit?

## Existing local evidence

- The user explicitly prefers cleaner, less slippery movement, deliberate recoil/braking and conventional controls. This is stronger evidence for our controller than another game’s marketing language.
- Current prototype: seven in-engine suites, 299 assertions; 4K screenshots inspected for the actual game, settings, bindings and suppression field. This supports mechanics/layout claims, not multiplayer enjoyment.
- Prior *Hidden-Reality Social Horror* workspace notes: separation as a trade-off and bounded contradictory information. The new notebook distills the relevant principles so no sibling repository is required.
- Primary Godot engine release `4.7.2-stable` and the Windows ZIP SHA-256 were checked through GitHub’s release API for the setup script. This is tooling provenance, not game-design evidence.

## Limits and next evidence

Most comparables were inspected through primary text, not played or exhaustively watched. No competitor art is copied into this repository. External image retrieval was unreliable, so actual visual review used this prototype’s renders; the next research pass should inspect a few official gameplay sequences before making precise claims about timing, HUDs or animation. No popularity ranking is used as proof that a mechanic will transfer.

Next questions: do people voluntarily exchange charge; do they understand its custody; does first-person speed prevent recognizing another player; does the group ever revise an accusation based on an observed physical event? Those need playtesting.
