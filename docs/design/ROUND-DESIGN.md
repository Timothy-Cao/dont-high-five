# Three possible games

All numbers below are tuning hypotheses. No hidden-role round is implemented.

| Direction | Thirty-second loop | Why high-five belongs | Main risk | First test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Blackout Relay — recommended** | Find a fuse, take a route, negotiate a handoff, power a circuit | Cargo transfer and assistance create trust with a visible benefit | Tasks become errands; permanent pairs solve the game | Six players, three circuits, one tamper tool |
| **Bad Battery — party fallback** | Pursue a carrier, accept a helpful charge, discover it is overheating, pass it on | Passing danger is the entire game | Nobody accepts once the rules are understood | Four players, public timer, transferable benefit and danger |
| **Palm Pals — cooperative extraction** | One anchors, one carries, both improvise a safe route | High-five joins a temporary cooperative traversal system | One skilled player drags everyone; sabotage is unnecessary | Two-player assist with recipient-controlled release |

Relay best fits the requested Lockdown/Among Us direction. Bad Battery is the cheapest way to learn whether the social hand gesture survives real latency. Palm Pals is a serious alternative if cooperative rescues produce better stories than deception.

## Proposed Blackout Relay round

**Premise:** After closing, six attraction robots have eight minutes to power three circuits and reopen the loading door. Five want to leave together. One wants the shutdown to finish. Everybody has the same visible body, hands and basic movement. The saboteur must also perform legitimate useful actions to maintain trust.

**Good win:** energize three circuits, then keep the exit powered for a 20-second departure window with at least one good player escaping. Escape does not require every player to stand on a pad; hostage tactics must not soft-lock victory. Consider a separate rescue score, not a requirement that forces abandonment into a loss.

**Bad win:** the timer expires before a good extraction. Disabling every good robot for a continuous short interval is an optional secondary ending, not required in the first experiment. We can test the timer-and-sabotage game without lethal combat first.

**Circuit step:** find its chunky fuse, physically carry it to an upper or lower receiver, then transfer a station-generated charge through a willing partner into the fuse. The carrier uses one hand to hold cargo and retains the other for movement. There is a slower ramp route for every delivery. A wall sling is faster but requires handing the fuse to someone else or placing it down first.

**Cooperative action:** a receiver cannot be charged twice by the same robot back-to-back. This is a proposed equipment interlock, public to everyone. It creates one purposeful exchange without demanding a high-five every few seconds. It is also the first rule to remove if players find it artificial.

**Sabotage:** an unsealed receiver can be put into a short fault state, or a carried charge can be corrupted. A corrupted charge still completes the visible handoff; 12 seconds later the receiver faults rather than banking progress. A wash station can inspect/clean a charge before delivery. Completed circuits stay completed: disruption adds a bounded repair action rather than erasing three minutes of work.

**Counterplay:** another robot can inspect the receiver’s last two deposits and their timestamps. This reveals custody, not alignment or the exact moment corruption happened. A player can have carried a bad charge unknowingly. The receipt must always be factually true; deception comes from incomplete context, never fabricated UI history.

**No early permanent elimination in the first rules test.** A robot can be knocked down, disarmed, rescued or reboot after a delay. This gives combat consequences while protecting the social session. If players keep attacking indiscriminately, reduce attack reward and increase witness consequences before adding a punishment meter.

## Opening, middle and finish

| Phase | Player experience | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 45 seconds | Everyone sees one charge transfer and chooses a task board route | Teach one social action before splitting |
| Next 2–3 minutes | Retrieve fuses from different heights; form pairs; exchange a charge | Let movement and cooperation create useful memories |
| Middle | One bounded blackout or corrupted receiver; rescue or inspect | Turn route choices into evidence and disagreements |
| Final 90 seconds | Fewer tasks remain; choose whether to regroup or push a risky delivery | Convert social decisions into a physical chase |
| Departure/reveal | Short extraction, then a chronological event recap | Explain what happened and invite the next round |

Avoid a mandatory meeting teleport in the first test. Let players talk and move at the same time, closer to Lockdown’s advertised continuous action. A later central alarm can be a voluntary gathering point with a cost, not a screen that repeatedly interrupts traversal. [Lockdown source](SOURCES.md#s01)

## Good-player agency

The good role should offer verbs before it offers chores:

- **Route:** take the bright ramp with witnesses or the faster, exposed sling route.
- **Delegate:** carry the fuse, scout the receiver, bring a charge, or guard a partner’s landing.
- **Verify:** spend time washing a charge or checking custody before accusing its last carrier.
- **Rescue:** give up a shortcut to retrieve a downed robot or their dropped cargo.
- **Defend:** break an incoming grip, use cover, redirect a chase through a field, or disarm at close range.
- **Commit:** keep the group safe at the cost of progress, or trust one partner to finish elsewhere.

Avoid a permanent support job that means watching a monitor while everybody else slingshots. Any information station should be a quick visit with a portable result.

## Antagonist advantages

| Proposed tool | Advantage | Tell/cost | Good response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Corrupt one carried charge | Delayed fault lets the culprit leave before discovery | One stored use; needs direct access; cleaning removes it | Inspect custody and wash suspicious cargo |
| Trip a local breaker | Create a short dark crossing and a plausible repair visit | Nearby relay buzz; fixed duration; cannot chain indefinitely | Alternate bright route or restore from either side |
| Emergency overclock | Briefly escape a pursuer or win a single contested handoff | Loud red arm flare; consumes the same scarce cartridge anyone can find | Notice use, pursue to cooldown, contest cartridges |
| Re-route one service shutter | Change the likely witness path | Mechanical noise, map lamp changes, always another exit | Follow the longer bypass; check which route was actually closed |

The killer’s advantage should be **preparation, information and timing**, not permanent immunity to the map’s rules. Start with corruption only. Add one escape tool if discovery currently means immediate, uninteresting defeat.

## Combat should preserve movement and inference

The existing two-fist attack is a movement/combat test against dummies. Its current 30 damage per fist and Overdrive burst are not multiplayer balance decisions. Do not reuse the 105-damage boosted burst as a surprise one-shot on players.

Proposed PvP: a fist hit costs a small amount of integrity and nudges, with a short stagger immunity window. Two good players coordinating should be able to defend someone, but a player should be able to escape a 1v1 through route choice. Prevent repeated tethering from taking control away indefinitely. Punches remain visible fists; a high-five remains an open palm with bilateral acceptance.

Found tools should produce new options, not simply bigger DPS: a one-use tether cutter, a foam shield, a signal decoy, or a wash cartridge. A dangerous weapon occupies a hand and is visible on the body when stowed. This costs mobility and supports observation. Avoid hidden shotguns deciding the opening loot race. ## Movement and level integration

The full building is 304 × 240 m. At base 7 m/s, a straight 240 m crossing takes about 34 seconds before turns, falls or searches; a 304 m crossing takes about 43 seconds. These are lower-bound arithmetic, not measured route times. In an eight-minute round, repeated uninformed crossings can consume the session.

For the first social test, activate three adjacent districts, not every distant corner. Use closed shutters and obvious sector lighting; keep the rest available in free play. Objectives should produce encounters around landings, turns and handoff ledges. Put their clues where players briefly slow down, not where they must read text at 40 m/s.

Every mandatory objective needs a walkable route. Movement mastery should shorten a trip or improve escape, not decide whether a new player can participate. Portals must show a shared destination identity, keep a small audible arrival cue, and never secretly send different players to arbitrary places in the first version.

## Dominant strategies to challenge

| Strategy | Why it could win too easily | Candidate response | What not to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Everyone stays together | Perfect witnesses defeat a lone saboteur | Several concurrent routes make grouped delivery slower; sabotage still costs time | Arbitrarily damage groups standing close together |
| Permanent trusted pairs | Pair identity solves every later decision | Cargo and receiver location rotate; short-lived help crosses pairs | Force random partner matching every minute |
| Never high-five | Removes the main risk entirely | Make clean transfers plainly valuable, and a slower safe route possible | Randomly infect players for refusing |
| Shoot everyone | Mechanical combat overwhelms deduction | Nonlethal first test, visible weapons, rescue and lost productivity | Rely solely on a tutorial saying “be nice” |
| Camp power station | Central Overdrive becomes the entire match | Announced spawn windows, two approach heights, portable partial alternatives | Add more raw damage to break camping |
| Hide forever in field room | Safe stalling until timer resolves | No objective progress inside, two observable exits | Let the killer silently ignore every field |
| Grapple away forever | Chases never conclude | Limited contact range, readable recovery and objective deadlines | Make basic movement feel bad everywhere |

We should test these strategies intentionally. A beautiful map does not resolve incentive problems.
