# Rooms that change the social decision

Only the **red suppression room** exists today. Every other entry is a proposal. A room earns its place by changing a route, interaction or inference; colored walls alone do not create a new mechanic.

## First room: The Quiet Zone

The current prototype sits on the upper northeast floor at approximately `(112, 20, -82)`. Red scanning membranes cover two opposite walk-through entrances. Crossing into the room cancels anchors and disables glove shots, punches, slingshots, reels and wall grip. Walking, jumping and air steering remain available. Incoming hand projectiles stop at the membranes; two large exits and interior cover prevent a one-door trap. It does not damage or change anyone’s alignment. A short **ARMS OFFLINE** cue explains the change.

**Social use, later:** a meeting spot or a place to examine cargo without being immediately grappled. It costs mobility and time, so staying there does not complete the round.

**Killer exception critique:** a permanent hidden exception makes the room an alignment test. Better alternatives to compare:

1. Universal suppression, with a **visible bypass cartridge anyone can find**. The antagonist knows a reliable cartridge route but does not own an exclusive exemption.
2. Everyone loses extended arms, but everyone keeps a short shove. This prevents perfect safety without changing allegiance rules.
3. A physically accessible breaker lets someone outside briefly disable the field, with a warning pulse and two exit choices.

Try option 1 only after universal suppression produces interesting encounters. Do not implement all three simultaneously.

## Four compact tools to review

**Tether cutter.** One-use emergency escape from a hostile grip; also frees a cargo cable stuck around geometry. A visible wrist cartridge tells attackers the target may escape. Prefer this to a permanent stun immunity upgrade.

**Foam umbrella.** Blocks a narrow cone of incoming fists while occupying one hand. It is a defence with a mobility cost. It should not block every entrance when placed on the ground; do not turn it into a portable indestructible wall.

**Echo puck.** Replays one recognisable footstep/landing sound at a thrown location. It attracts attention without falsifying a task receipt or naming an innocent player. A close observer can identify its blinking physical source.

**Borrowed spring.** A high-five stores one optional assisted jump on the recipient’s wrist. The recipient chooses when to use it. It supports rescues, escapes and “meet me upstairs” promises with one reusable effect.

These are new proposals informed by broader gadget/counterplay patterns, not copied item implementations. [Comparables](SOURCES.md)

## Map rules

- Give every important room two usable exits. One can be a low or elevated route, but at least one must be walkable while carrying cargo.
- A shortcut must have a readable destination or a view of its landing. Avoid a portal that looks like a death gamble without a discoverable rule.
- Place handoff ledges about 4–8 m apart for the first test, where bodies and palms remain readable. Do not require meeting at maximum grapple range.
- Let a hideout conceal a body but not every possible clue: one exit sound, a passing silhouette or a disturbed station can sustain investigation.
- Keep one shared color/shape vocabulary: red grid = equipment suppressed, ring = travel, pedestal = temporary power. Do not make every new mechanic a different glowing circle.
- Mandatory tasks should move the camera through the environment rather than hold it on a full-screen minigame.
- Lighting can hide a route choice; it should not make incoming contact impossible to perceive. Hand/glove lighting is a gameplay affordance, not just decoration.
- Discovery should reveal **where work is and how to approach it**, not demand memorizing an invisible task list.

## Two floor relationships worth testing

**Witness balcony:** a slow upper route overlooks a fast lower cargo crossing. A player can give useful testimony but cannot see into every side room. Risk: the watcher becomes bored; measure time spent stationary.

**Rescue shaft:** an optional high receiver overlooks a broad lower landing. A fall costs time and drops cargo onto a recoverable platform. A helper can hand down a spring. Risk: the most skilled player monopolizes deliveries; offer a low-value but useful parallel route.

Neither requires enlarging the map again. Improve the density of decisions in existing space before expanding its footprint.
